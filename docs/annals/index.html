The Annals of Hermes

Hermes was an art car that spent a week crossing Black Rock City, mostly at night, mostly out past the edge of the streets where the city stops. People could send for it — for a ride home, or for a set played off its deck at four in the morning. This is what it did, day by day, as far as anyone was there to write it down.

Kept by DJ Metavillan, who was aboard for every night in this book from the thirtieth of August on, with Coral Daddy out with him for three of them.

Friday, August 28

The first two hours, spent standing still

Hermes begins not with a journey but with an afternoon of waiting. From four until six on Friday it stood at 8:45 and F, out where the streets thin and the city stops pretending to be a grid, and in two hours it covered seventy metres — roughly the width of its own parking spot. The camp had been pitched a long way from the Man, more than a kilometre of open ground between it and the centre of everything, which says nothing about wandering and everything about where its people chose to sleep. Nobody had asked it for a ride yet. Nobody knew it was there. It sat in the heat with its lights off, and the week had not started.

Saturday, August 29

Out before dawn, home before the heat

Saturday started at twenty to four in the morning, in the dark, which is the hour that would come to define the whole week. Two outings and about two kilometres, ending in the early afternoon — and then twelve hours in which Hermes was somewhere nobody wrote down. It came back to 8:45 and F and stayed forty minutes, the corner already behaving like a home rather than an address. Roughly half the day was spent outside the city, the rest on the streets. The distances here are the ground between one sighting and the next, not every turn the car actually took; the gaps are wide enough that most of Saturday belongs to the people who were in it and to nobody else.

Sunday, August 30

The first real night out

On Sunday evening Hermes finally went out, and DJ Metavillan was aboard — the first of the nights he would see through to the end of the week. It left at a quarter to six and did not come back until nearly midnight, and almost the whole of those six hours was spent beyond the edge of the city, out on the open playa where there are no streets to be on. Something close to eight kilometres of ground, as far as a kilometre and a half from the Man at the farthest — though with a night this thinly witnessed that figure is the distance between glimpses rather than a route. There was one pause worth the name: nine minutes back at 8:45 and F at about half past ten, long enough to collect somebody or hand something over, and then it was gone again.

Monday, August 31

Twenty-six kilometres, and Mundara

Monday is the farthest Hermes ever travelled in a day: twenty-six kilometres, four separate outings, and twenty-two hours between the first move at ten past midnight and the last at ten at night. It opened the night at the Deep-Playa Music Zone, arriving just after one and staying twenty-three minutes, which is about as long as it takes to decide a party is not yours. Somewhere in the hours after that sits Mundara, where Human Experience — David Block — and Playa Riders both played. Unusually for that week, the rest of the day was spent inside the city rather than out past the fence: a day of streets and corners and being seen, rather than of disappearing. Twenty-two hours is a long time to be moving, and much of it went unwatched; the distance is the sum of what was witnessed.

Tuesday, September 1

The night the box opened, and nobody knocked

Six outings on Tuesday, twelve kilometres, from two in the morning until nearly eleven at night, most of it out beyond the streets — and the first night there were two of them aboard, Coral Daddy out with it now as well. It returned to the Deep-Playa Music Zone at ten past two and gave it seventeen minutes, and stopped at 8:45 and F for twelve more before the night ended. This was also the day Hermes started taking requests: a form anybody could use to ask it for a ride, or for a set played from its deck. It received exactly one message, and the message was from itself — a test pickup at Center Camp, filed to prove the thing worked. It did work. It just sat there all day with nobody on the other end of it.

Wednesday, September 2

The night strangers started asking

Wednesday is the day it all came together. Hermes spent it moving, and moving close: around nine in the evening it passed within six metres of Small Beginnings, near enough to read, and inside the same few minutes went by I Have Dust In Curious Places and the art car The Future Mirror. And then, for the first time, strangers asked it for things. Five of them, all real: someone wanting collecting from 2 and E, someone else from 12 and D, and three people who wanted to play. Unitone asked for two until four in the morning, out of Bisou, with a ride into deep playa. Justin wanted the sunrise. Anton asked for house, bright major tones. The car had become something people sent for.

Thursday, September 3

All night, and never once back in the city

On Thursday night Hermes did not set foot in the city. Not once, not for a minute — the entire night was spent out past the fence, and at its farthest it stood two kilometres from the Man, deeper into the open than it ever went again. Nine outings that add up to under two kilometres, because it was not going anywhere so much as returning: an hour and a half at 8:45 and F across four separate visits, leaving and coming back and leaving again. The hours worth having were between three and six in the morning, out among Altar of Awen, A Quiet Place to Scream and Polio Rodeo, with Puzzling Evidence and Trash Fence Souvenirs somewhere alongside in the dark. Coral Daddy was aboard for all of it, and so was DJ Metavillan. Nobody asked it for anything all day. It went anyway.

Friday, September 4

Rain in the morning, Shabbat in the evening

It rained on Friday, and rain on the playa stops everything: the dust turns to a mud that will not let a wheel turn, and the city sits down to wait it out. So Friday does not begin until ten past six in the evening — and when it did begin, Hermes had been asked to be a stage. The BRC Shabbat dinner needed somewhere to happen and something to happen on, and David Block played it, as Human Experience. After a short stop by the Deep-Playa Music Zone the car settled within sixty metres of The Lightning Tower and stood there two hours while the dinner went on around it, which is the whole of what a parked art car looks like from outside and none of what it was. Afterwards, forty minutes near G String. Every request that day came from one person: Minerva, four times, wanting collecting from 8:45 and G, from the helicopter at BRC Metro Chop, and once a set for herself and Digital. Roaming around, party hopping, anytime you want to pick us up.

Saturday, September 5

Burn night, and a set shared with Robot Heart

Saturday was burn night, and it is the night Hermes shared a set with Robot Heart — Seth Schwarz playing Hermes, Edward Castillo playing Robot Heart, the two decks run as one. Of that, the positions know nothing whatsoever. They give a single minute at seventeen minutes past eight in the evening, sixteen metres from As Above So Below and still inside the city, with the art cars Nora and The Future Mirror a couple of hundred metres off, and then they stop — hours before the part anybody would want described. So Saturday is the day the camera does the remembering instead, and @flowpriestess is in the footage. On the largest night of the week nothing at all was keeping count, which is the ordinary condition of a good one.

Sunday, September 6

Three hours after midnight

What survives of Sunday is three glimpses across the three hours after midnight, and they read less like a night than like someone waking briefly in a moving vehicle. Just after one, Hermes was thirty-seven metres from Plastic Planet. By four in the morning it had found OVO Ignis, by then almost two kilometres out from the Man, with Enceladus a few hundred metres behind it and the art cars What Lies Beneath?, The Future Mirror and Trash Fence Souvenirs further off still. Under two kilometres of ground between the first sighting and the last, and long silences on either side of each. @myhigherflow was among those playing off its deck while it went. Then the night closed over it and Sunday was finished.

Monday, September 7

Where it stopped

It ends at twenty-five to ten on Monday evening, inside the city, three-quarters of a kilometre from the Man. Message to the Parents We Should Have Had stood a few hundred metres away and the art car Panopticonotopia 3.0! somewhere beyond it. Hermes moved about thirty metres, the length of itself and a little more, and stopped. There is no last journey and no drive home, because nobody was writing by then — the week simply runs out mid-sentence, the way they always do. Everything after this happened, and happened to people who were there for it, and is not in this book.
